# Landing page recommendations

## Current impression

Thido currently sends a first-time visitor directly to the new-document workflow. That is a strong product decision: the visitor can begin immediately instead of reading a long marketing page. The page feels sparse because it presents the brand, one sentence, and five empty fields without first showing what Thido can produce or reducing the uncertainty involved in starting.

The best improvement is not to add a large conventional marketing page. Keep the form as the main event and add a small amount of context, proof, and guidance around it.

Visitors can choose between a guided questionnaire and a freeform prompt. Guided mode is useful when they want help shaping the brief; freeform mode is for visitors who already know exactly what they want to request.

## Recommended first pass

### 1. Add a useful header action

Keep the Thido wordmark on the left and add **Sign in** on the right. An authenticated visitor can instead see **My documents**. This gives the wide header a purpose and gives returning users an obvious route into their work.

### 2. Add document starters

Place a compact dropdown beneath the introduction with more than ten common document types, including proposals, client updates, strategy memos, briefs, recaps, requirements, business cases, research summaries, articles, press releases, grant applications, policy briefs, and job descriptions.

Selecting one should replace all five outline answers without immediately submitting the form. Make that replacement behavior explicit beside the control so a visitor is not surprised when changing templates.

### 3. Add one reassurance line near Generate

Use a quiet, concise note such as:

> Start without an account. We will ask you to sign in when you generate your draft.

This explains the guest-first flow at the moment it matters and removes uncertainty about whether entering text creates an account or saves data remotely.

### 4. Show what happens next

Below the form, add a compact three-step strip:

1. Share what you know.
2. Answer a few focused follow-ups.
3. Edit and refine your first draft.

This makes the five questions feel like the beginning of a short process rather than an open-ended questionnaire.

### 5. Add a minimal footer

Include links to **Privacy**, **Terms**, and **Sign in**, plus a short line such as “Your words stay yours.” Only make claims that are supported by the privacy policy and actual data handling.

## Suggested page order

1. Header: Thido, Sign in/My documents
2. Hero copy
3. Guided/freeform mode switch
4. Starter dropdown and five-question form in guided mode, or one prompt field in freeform mode
5. Guest-flow reassurance and Generate button
6. Three-step explanation that applies to both paths
7. Minimal legal footer

## What not to add yet

- Invented testimonials, customer logos, usage counts, or performance claims
- A large feature grid before visitors can start writing
- Animated gradients or generic AI imagery that compete with the document workflow
- Starter categories that do not provide complete answers for all five outline questions
- A second primary call to action with different wording

## Priority

The header action, document starters, reassurance line, and three-step strip add enough context without competing with the form. Keep the page wide and direct rather than surrounding the workflow with decorative product mockups.
